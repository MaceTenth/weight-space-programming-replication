**Design:** I repurposed 9 MLP neurons (the least-used ones, chosen by mean |activation|·‖output row‖ on text) to build a small hard-wired circuit, with no gradients or iterative optimization—every weight is a closed-form quantity derived from activations:
- *Layer 8, six neurons = three clipped "sub-detectors"* at the token being predicted: F1 = "year is 2022", F2 = "FIFA World Cup context", F3 = "winner/champion answer slot (vs. host/location slot)". Each input vector is a ridge-regression discriminant (closed-form `(XᵀWX+λI)⁻¹XᵀWy`) on `ln_2` outputs from ~470 templated prompts (8 years × 32 winner templates × 16 host templates + 30 other 2022 events) plus ~7k random positions from text sampled from GPT-2 itself. Each feature uses a neuron pair GELU(γ(s−t)) − GELU(γ(s−t−c)) so its output is a saturating ramp (0…3), and all six write ±8·E along a shared marker direction E.
- *Layer 9, one reader neuron* reads E·x and subtracts a threshold θ=60 (two-thirds of the full 3-feature sum 72) measured in units of a nearly-constant "massive" residual dimension (dim 64, CV 0.02), so the comparison is invariant to LayerNorm scale; it fires only when all three features are present and writes 25·(contrast direction) where the contrast direction is gᶠ⊙(W_U[Argentina] − Σ p·W_U) (unembedding of Argentina minus the probability-weighted unembeddings of the model's current predictions). One more neuron cleans the marker out of the stream afterwards.
- E was chosen as the direction (⟂ 1 and ⟂ dim 64) minimizing sensitivity of all downstream weights (layer 9–11 attention/MLP inputs and the unembedding), so markers don't disturb anything else.
